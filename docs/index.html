How to read this

  • Observed NISAR L-band radar backscatter or phase, as delivered by NASA/ASF.
  • Derived Computed by us from NISAR (thresholds, differences, hectares).
  • Context Non-radar layers: optical basemap, places, NASA POWER rain.
  • Hypothesis Possible physical drivers. Not proven by this tool.
Why radar here?

NISAR sends its own microwave pulses, so it images day and night, every 12 days, through cloud and dust. Radar is sensitive to roughness and moisture: calm brine in evaporation ponds reflects the pulse away (very dark), rough salt crust and built structures send it back (bright), and wetter soil in the vegas (high-Andean wetlands) raises backscatter.

Limits (please read)
  • Smooth salt crust can be as dark as brine. Check the optical basemap and the change between dates before calling something a pond.
  • Only compare dates from the same track, direction and data maturity (the app warns you).
  • A single 12-day interferogram is not a subsidence rate; atmospheric delay over the Andes can exceed mm-level signals.
  • Rain is a coarse (~50 km) NASA POWER estimate at the AOI centre, for context only.
Hypotheses we test
  • Hypothesis Growth of low-backscatter area inside pond fields between dates reflects expansion of brine evaporation ponds.
  • Hypothesis Backscatter drops in vegas outside the rainy season (Dec-Mar) would point to non-climatic drivers worth field verification.
